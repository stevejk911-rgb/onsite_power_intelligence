Power and land are no longer the only things that kill a data center. The community is. The projects below did not fail on economics. They failed on water, on a paperwork defect, on a tax break the public voted away. The pattern repeats. Here are the autopsies.

Autopsy 01Dead

PW Digital Gateway

One of the largest campuses ever proposed. A $24.7B plan killed by a paperwork defect that was checkable from day one.

Where · Prince William County, VA Who · QTS + Compass Datacenters Scale · 2,100+ acres · 37 data centers · 23M sq ft Value · $24.7B / $400.5M yr tax (county est.) Approved · 4-3-1 vote, Dec 2023 Died · Jul 2, 2026
Cause of death

Not water, not the grid, not economics. A public-notice defect in the county's own rezoning. The rezoning passed on a razor-thin 4-3-1 board vote after a 29-hour hearing, then opponents sued on process, not merits. A judge found the county published newspaper notices at the wrong intervals, failed to make the ordinances available for inspection, and held the hearing too soon after final notice. The rezoning was voided in 2025, the Court of Appeals affirmed on Mar 31, 2026, and QTS dropped its final appeal on Jul 2, 2026.

The 4 fatal mistakes
  1. Built on a cracked legal foundation. A $24.7B, multi-year effort rested on a rezoning with notice defects. One litigable flaw, one point of failure, never stress-tested.
  2. Maximized opposition intensity. A 29-hour hostile hearing and a 4-3-1 split produced a motivated, litigation-capable opposition, plus the public record it used to win.
  3. No off-ramp, no phasing. One all-or-nothing rezoning across 2,100+ acres. When the one thing broke, everything broke.
  4. Misread the opponent. This was a disciplined legal campaign. The developers brought a construction plan to a courtroom fight.
Signals that sat in the public record before it died
  • A 4-3-1 approval with one abstention: a fragile political mandate, not a durable one.
  • A 29-hour hearing with organized, litigation-ready opponents who filed suit almost immediately.
  • The notice-compliance record itself, a public procedural checklist any counsel could have audited before the land spend.
What would have saved it
  • A procedural pre-mortem: verify every notice and hearing-timing rule is bulletproof before capital goes in. What killed this was findable in advance.
  • Phase and scale the ask to remove the all-or-nothing failure mode.
  • Concessions that make suing not worth it: benefits, buffers, on-site power to defuse the ratepayer argument.
The question for your dealYour approval is only as strong as its weakest procedural link and its most motivated opponent. Do you know where both are, before the capital goes in?

Sources: Court of Appeals of Virginia; InsideNoVa; Prince William Times; Virginia Business; Data Center Frontier.

Autopsy 02Rejected, then revived

Project Blue

Rejected 7-0 by the city, then revived one jurisdiction over. The comeback is the real lesson.

Where · near Tucson, Pima County, AZ Who · Beale Infrastructure (Blue Owl); AWS reported Size · 600 MW · ~$3.6B · 290 acres Water · up to ~283M gallons/yr reported (~3,000 homes) City vote · 7-0 reject, Aug 6, 2025 Revived · county deal 3-2, Dec 16, 2025
Cause of the rejection

Water, secrecy, and a jurisdiction that could say no. The original design needed City of Tucson water, and annexation to get it, in an arid, drought-anxious region. Reported draw ran up to roughly 283 million gallons a year, about 3,000 households, while the end user stayed undisclosed. That handed opponents a clean "corporate water grab, in secret" story, and the city council had the votes to reject it 7-0.

The 4 fatal mistakes that lost the city
  1. Picked a water fight it did not need. Designing around city water in a drought region made the most inflammable objection possible.
  2. Secrecy backfired. Hiding the end user converted uncertainty into distrust.
  3. Chose a jurisdiction that could veto. A politically exposed city council is a single winnable target.
  4. Led with the ask, not the concessions. No water-neutrality or benefit story arrived before the opposition did.
Signals that sat in the public record before the vote
  • A water-dependent design in a metro already fighting over supply.
  • An undisclosed end user, reliably read by communities as something to hide.
  • Approval that required annexation and a council vote: a discretionary gate, not a right.
The comeback, which is the antidote
  • Removed the fatal dependency: switched to closed-loop cooling, which killed the "water grab" argument at the root.
  • Changed the venue: moved to Pima County, where a 3-2 board vote approved a binding agreement (with independent annual verification that 100% of energy is matched with renewables), and closed on the land in December 2025.
  • Locked power early through the Arizona Corporation Commission, removing the energy-uncertainty attack.
The question for your dealWhat single resource dependency, water, city power, or annexation, is your project's kill switch, and are you engineering it out before opponents find it?

Sources: AZ Luminaria; AZPM; KJZZ; Tucson Sentinel; Democracy Now!; DataCenterDynamics. AWS end user per reporting; sponsors did not confirm. Water figure as reported.

Autopsy 03National template

Port Washington

Opponents did not fight the zoning. They put the developer's tax incentives to a public vote, and handed every town in America the template.

Where · Port Washington, WI Who · Vantage; land via Cloverleaf Infrastructure Scale · up to 3.5 GW reported Referendum · ~66% yes, Apr 7, 2026 Rule · any incentive over $10M needs a public vote First · anti-DC referendum in the U.S.
Cause of death

Not the zoning, the financing assumption. Big deals lean on tax-increment financing and local tax breaks. Opponents went to the ballot box and made any incentive over $10M require a public vote, turning the developer's economic model into a referendum they were nearly certain to win. It passed roughly 66%, and organizers pitched it openly as a national model.

The 4 fatal mistakes
  1. Depended on discretionary incentives, a lever the resentful public can yank away.
  2. Fought the last war (zoning) and lost the new one (the ballot). Opposition invented a new instrument while the developer was still playing permitting.
  3. Let "costs vs. benefits" become the frame. Once about 70% of voters believe costs outweigh benefits, every ballot mechanism turns hostile.
  4. No local economic narrative that survived a vote. Nothing made residents feel they gained enough to approve the subsidy.
Signals that sat in the public record before the vote
  • An economic model that leaned on a discretionary tax break a local body could revoke.
  • Marquette polling already moving against it: statewide "costs outweigh benefits" climbed from 55% to 70%.
  • A ballot mechanism available to organizers, waiting to be used.
What would have saved it
  • Do not build the model on incentives residents can revoke, or lock them before opposition organizes.
  • Win "costs vs. benefits" early with concrete per-household benefits (bill relief, a community fund) before the number crosses 50%.
  • Assume the ballot box is now a venue. Referendums are contagious.
The question for your dealIf your economics depend on a tax break, could a citizen vote take it away, and have you already lost the "costs vs. benefits" argument in that community?

Sources: NOTUS; Wisconsin Watch; WPR; FOX6 Milwaukee; Marquette Law School Poll. Campus scale reported up to 3.5 GW; figures per public reporting.

The pattern

Three deaths, one anatomy.

Different states, different triggers, the same four failure modes. Every project that dies has at least one. Every project that survives designs them out.

MODE 1
A fatal dependency
Water, city power, annexation, or a tax break you do not control. The dependency is the vulnerability.
MODE 2
A veto point
An elected body that can say no, a rezoning that can be challenged, a ballot that can be run. A single winnable target.
MODE 3
The "costs vs. benefits" frame is lost
Once residents believe the project costs them more than it gives, every mechanism turns against you.
MODE 4
Concessions arrived late
Benefits offered after opposition organized read as damage control, not good faith. Timing is everything.
